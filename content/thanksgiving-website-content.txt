1.1 HERB-BUTTER ROAST TURKEY
----------------------------------------------------------------
Serves: 10-12  |  Prep: 30 minutes (plus 1-2 days dry-brining)
Cook: about 3 to 3 1/2 hours  |  Rest: 30-40 minutes

A dry brine seasons the meat all the way through and gives you
crisp, golden skin without the mess of a bucket of salt water.
Herb butter tucked under the skin keeps the breast juicy.

INGREDIENTS
Dry brine
  - 1 whole turkey, 12-14 lb (5.5-6.5 kg), fully thawed
  - 3 tablespoons kosher salt (use 2 tablespoons if using
    table salt)
  - 1 tablespoon light brown sugar
  - 2 teaspoons freshly ground black pepper
  - 1 teaspoon dried thyme
  - Finely grated zest of 1 orange

Herb butter
  - 1 cup (2 sticks / 225 g) unsalted butter, softened
  - 2 tablespoons fresh sage, finely chopped
  - 2 tablespoons fresh thyme leaves
  - 1 tablespoon fresh rosemary, finely chopped
  - 3 garlic cloves, finely grated
  - 1/2 teaspoon black pepper

For the pan
  - 2 onions, quartered
  - 2 carrots, cut into chunks
  - 2 celery stalks, cut into chunks
  - 1 head garlic, halved crosswise
  - 1 orange, quartered (the one you zested)
  - A small bunch of fresh herbs (sage, thyme, rosemary)
  - 2 cups (480 ml) chicken or turkey stock

METHOD
1. Dry brine (1-2 days ahead). Remove the neck and giblets from
   the cavities (save them for gravy). Pat the turkey very dry
   with paper towels. Mix the salt, sugar, pepper, dried thyme
   and orange zest. Sprinkle evenly over every surface of the
   bird, including inside the cavity. Set the turkey on a rack
   over a rimmed tray and refrigerate uncovered for 24-48 hours.
   The skin will look dry and slightly translucent; that is
   exactly what you want.

2. Bring to temperature. Take the turkey out of the
   refrigerator 1 hour before roasting. Do not rinse it.
   Heat the oven to 425°F (220°C) with a rack in the lower third.

3. Make the herb butter. Mash the butter with the chopped herbs,
   garlic and pepper until evenly combined.

4. Butter the bird. Starting at the cavity end, slide your
   fingers gently between the skin and the breast meat to
   loosen it, working carefully so the skin does not tear.
   Push about two-thirds of the herb butter under the skin and
   massage it over the breast and as far down the thighs as you
   can reach. Rub the remaining butter over the outside.

5. Prepare the pan. Scatter the onions, carrots, celery and
   halved garlic in a large roasting pan. Stuff the turkey
   cavity loosely with the orange quarters and herb bunch.
   Tuck the wing tips behind the back and tie the legs together
   loosely with kitchen twine. Set the turkey on a rack over the
   vegetables (or directly on the vegetables if you have no
   rack). Pour the stock into the pan.

6. Roast. Roast at 425°F (220°C) for 30 minutes to start
   browning the skin. Reduce the oven to 325°F (165°C) and
   continue roasting, about 2 1/2 to 3 hours more for a
   12-14 lb bird. Add a splash of water or stock to the pan if
   it threatens to dry out. If the breast browns too quickly,
   cover it loosely with foil.

7. Check doneness. Begin checking with an instant-read
   thermometer about 30 minutes before you expect it to be done.
   The turkey is done when the thickest part of the thigh
   (not touching bone) reads 165°F (74°C) and the thickest part
   of the breast reads at least 165°F (74°C).

8. Rest. Transfer the turkey to a carving board, tent loosely
   with foil and rest for 30-40 minutes. This lets the juices
   settle and gives you time to make the gravy and reheat sides.
   Strain and save the pan drippings for gravy.

9. Carve and serve. See "Carving" in Section 2.

TIPS
  - No time to dry brine? Season generously with salt and pepper
    just before roasting. You will still get a good bird.
  - Every oven is different. Always trust the thermometer over
    the clock.

1.2 CLASSIC SAGE AND ONION STUFFING
----------------------------------------------------------------
Serves: 10-12  |  Prep: 30 minutes (plus bread drying)
Cook: 45-55 minutes

Baked in a dish rather than inside the bird, this stuffing gets a
crisp, buttery top and a soft, savory middle (and is easier to
cook safely).

INGREDIENTS
  - 1 1/2 lb (680 g) sturdy white bread, sourdough or French loaf,
    cut into 3/4-inch (2 cm) cubes (about 14-16 cups)
  - 1/2 cup (1 stick / 115 g) unsalted butter, plus extra for the
    dish
  - 2 large onions, diced
  - 4 celery stalks, diced
  - 4 garlic cloves, minced
  - 2 tablespoons fresh sage, chopped (or 2 teaspoons dried)
  - 1 tablespoon fresh thyme leaves (or 1 teaspoon dried)
  - 1/4 cup fresh flat-leaf parsley, chopped
  - 1 teaspoon kosher salt, plus more to taste
  - 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
  - 2 large eggs
  - 3 to 3 1/2 cups (720-840 ml) low-sodium chicken or turkey
    stock, warmed

METHOD
1. Dry the bread. Spread the bread cubes on two baking sheets.
   Either leave them out uncovered overnight, or bake at 300°F
   (150°C) for 25-30 minutes, stirring halfway, until dry and
   crisp but not browned. Cool and tip into a very large bowl.

2. Cook the vegetables. Melt the butter in a large skillet over
   medium heat. Add the onions and celery with a pinch of salt
   and cook, stirring occasionally, for 10-12 minutes until soft
   and translucent. Add the garlic, sage and thyme and cook for
   1 minute more until fragrant.

3. Combine. Scrape the vegetables and all the butter over the
   bread. Add the parsley, salt and pepper and toss well.

4. Moisten. Whisk the eggs with 3 cups of the warm stock. Pour
   over the bread and fold gently until the bread has absorbed
   the liquid. The mixture should be moist but not soggy; add
   more stock a splash at a time if there are dry patches.

5. Bake. Heat the oven to 375°F (190°C). Butter a 9x13-inch
   (23x33 cm) baking dish and spread the stuffing in it. Dot the
   top with a few small pieces of butter if you like. Cover with
   foil and bake for 25 minutes. Uncover and bake 20-30 minutes
   more until the top is golden and crisp and the center reaches
   165°F (74°C).

VARIATIONS
  - Sausage: Brown 1 lb (450 g) pork sausage, crumbled, before
    the vegetables; cook the vegetables in the fat, reducing the
    butter by half.
  - Apple and pecan: Add 2 diced tart apples with the celery and
    fold in 1 cup toasted chopped pecans.
  - Mushroom: Add 12 oz (340 g) sliced mushrooms to the skillet
    after the onions soften and cook until browned.

1.3 ORANGE-GINGER CRANBERRY SAUCE
----------------------------------------------------------------
Makes: about 3 cups (serves 10-12)  |  Prep: 5 minutes
Cook: 15 minutes  |  Chill: at least 2 hours

Bright, tart and barely sweet enough. It sets as it cools and
tastes even better made a few days ahead.

INGREDIENTS
  - 12 oz (340 g) fresh or frozen cranberries
  - 3/4 cup (150 g) granulated sugar
  - 1/2 cup (120 ml) fresh orange juice
  - 1/4 cup (60 ml) water
  - Finely grated zest of 1 orange
  - 1 tablespoon fresh ginger, finely grated
  - 1 cinnamon stick
  - Pinch of salt

METHOD
1. Combine everything except the zest in a medium saucepan.
2. Bring to a boil over medium-high heat, stirring until the
   sugar dissolves.
3. Reduce the heat to a lively simmer. Cook for 10-12 minutes,
   stirring often, until most of the berries have burst and the
   sauce has thickened. Press some berries against the side of
   the pan for a chunkier-but-saucy texture.
4. Remove from the heat, discard the cinnamon stick and stir in
   the orange zest. Taste and add a little more sugar if needed
   while it is still warm.
5. Cool to room temperature, then cover and refrigerate for at
   least 2 hours. It will thicken considerably as it chills.
   Keeps up to 10 days in the refrigerator.

VARIATIONS
  - Swap the sugar for 2/3 cup maple syrup.
  - Stir in 2 tablespoons orange liqueur or port at the end.
  - Add a handful of toasted chopped walnuts just before serving.

1.4 MAKE-AHEAD TURKEY GRAVY
----------------------------------------------------------------
Makes: about 4 cups  |  Prep: 15 minutes  |  Cook: 2 hours
(stock) plus 15 minutes (gravy)

Making the gravy base days ahead means no last-minute scramble.
On the day, you simply whisk in the pan drippings for full
roast-turkey flavor.

INGREDIENTS
Rich stock
  - 2 lb (900 g) turkey wings, drumsticks or necks (plus the neck
    and giblets from your bird, excluding the liver)
  - 1 tablespoon vegetable oil
  - 1 onion, quartered (skin on is fine)
  - 1 carrot, roughly chopped
  - 1 celery stalk, roughly chopped
  - 8 cups (2 liters) low-sodium chicken stock or water
  - A few sprigs of thyme and parsley, 1 bay leaf,
    6 black peppercorns

Gravy
  - 6 tablespoons (85 g) unsalted butter (or turkey fat skimmed
    from the drippings)
  - 1/2 cup (65 g) all-purpose flour
  - 4 cups (960 ml) rich turkey stock (from above)
  - Turkey pan drippings, fat skimmed off (optional, on the day)
  - 1 teaspoon fresh thyme leaves or 1 small sprig sage
  - Salt and black pepper to taste
  - 1 teaspoon soy sauce or Worcestershire sauce (optional, for
    depth)

METHOD
Make the stock (up to 3 days ahead, or freeze up to 3 months)
1. Heat the oven to 425°F (220°C). Toss the turkey parts, onion,
   carrot and celery with the oil on a sheet pan. Roast for
   45-60 minutes until deeply browned.
2. Transfer everything to a large pot. Pour a little water onto
   the hot sheet pan, scrape up the browned bits and add to the
   pot. Add the stock or water, herbs, bay leaf and peppercorns.
3. Bring to a boil, then lower to a gentle simmer for 1 1/2 to
   2 hours, skimming any foam. Strain, discard the solids, and
   cool. You should have about 5-6 cups. Refrigerate.

Make the gravy
4. Melt the butter in a saucepan over medium heat. Whisk in the
   flour and cook, whisking constantly, for 3-5 minutes until
   the roux is golden brown and smells nutty.
5. Gradually whisk in the warm stock, about 1 cup at a time,
   whisking until smooth after each addition.
6. Add the herbs and simmer for 8-10 minutes, whisking now and
   then, until thick enough to coat the back of a spoon.
   (At this stage you can cool and refrigerate the gravy for up
   to 3 days.)
7. On Thanksgiving: Reheat the gravy gently. While the turkey
   rests, pour the pan drippings into a fat separator or measuring
   cup and let the fat rise. Add the de-fatted juices to the
   gravy. Splash a little stock into the hot roasting pan,
   scrape up the browned bits and strain those in too.
8. Simmer for a few minutes, adjust the thickness with more stock
   if needed, and season with salt, pepper and soy sauce to taste.
   Remove any herb stems and strain for a perfectly smooth finish.

FIXES
  - Lumpy? Blend with an immersion blender or pour through a
    fine sieve.
  - Too thin? Whisk 1 tablespoon cornstarch into 2 tablespoons
    cold water and stir into the simmering gravy.
  - Too thick? Whisk in warm stock a little at a time.
  - Too salty? Add unsalted stock, or a splash of cream.

1.5 CREAMY GARLIC MASHED POTATOES
----------------------------------------------------------------
Serves: 10-12  |  Prep: 20 minutes  |  Cook: 30 minutes

Yukon Golds give a naturally buttery, creamy mash; warming the
dairy with garlic makes it extra smooth and flavorful.

INGREDIENTS
  - 5 lb (2.3 kg) Yukon Gold potatoes, peeled and cut into 2-inch
    (5 cm) chunks
  - 2 tablespoons kosher salt (for the cooking water)
  - 1 cup (240 ml) whole milk
  - 1 cup (240 ml) heavy cream
  - 6 garlic cloves, lightly smashed
  - 3/4 cup (1 1/2 sticks / 170 g) unsalted butter, cut into pieces
  - 1/2 cup (120 g) sour cream or cream cheese, at room temperature
  - Salt and white or black pepper to taste
  - 2 tablespoons fresh chives, chopped, to garnish

METHOD
1. Boil. Put the potatoes in a large pot and cover with cold
   water by 1 inch. Add the salt. Bring to a boil, then reduce to
   a steady simmer and cook 15-20 minutes until a knife slides
   into the chunks with no resistance.
2. Infuse the dairy. Meanwhile, warm the milk, cream and garlic
   in a small saucepan over low heat until steaming. Turn off the
   heat and let it steep. Remove the garlic cloves (or mash them
   in for a stronger flavor).
3. Dry. Drain the potatoes well, return them to the hot pot and
   set over low heat for 1-2 minutes, shaking the pot, to steam
   off excess moisture.
4. Mash. Mash with a potato masher or pass through a ricer for
   the smoothest result. Do not use a food processor or blender;
   it makes potatoes gluey.
5. Finish. Stir in the butter until melted, then the sour cream.
   Add the warm garlic milk gradually, stirring, until the
   potatoes are as creamy as you like (you may not need it all).
   Season well with salt and pepper. Top with chives and a pat of
   butter.

MAKE AHEAD
  - Make up to 2 days ahead. Spread in a buttered baking dish,
    cool, cover and refrigerate. Reheat covered at 350°F (175°C)
    for 30-40 minutes, stirring halfway and adding a splash of
    warm milk. Or keep warm in a slow cooker on LOW for up to
    4 hours.

1.6 FROM-SCRATCH GREEN BEAN CASSEROLE
----------------------------------------------------------------
Serves: 10-12  |  Prep: 30 minutes  |  Cook: 45 minutes

All the comfort of the classic, made with fresh beans, a real
mushroom cream sauce and homemade crispy shallots.

INGREDIENTS
Crispy shallots
  - 4 large shallots (or 1 large onion), very thinly sliced
    into rings
  - 1/3 cup (40 g) all-purpose flour
  - 1/2 teaspoon salt
  - About 1 cup (240 ml) vegetable oil for frying

Casserole
  - 2 lb (900 g) fresh green beans, trimmed and halved
  - 3 tablespoons unsalted butter
  - 12 oz (340 g) cremini mushrooms, sliced
  - 3 garlic cloves, minced
  - 1 teaspoon fresh thyme leaves
  - 3 tablespoons all-purpose flour
  - 1 cup (240 ml) low-sodium chicken or vegetable stock
  - 1 cup (240 ml) whole milk or half-and-half
  - 1/4 teaspoon ground nutmeg
  - 1 teaspoon soy sauce
  - Salt and black pepper to taste

METHOD
1. Make the crispy shallots. Toss the sliced shallots with the
   flour and salt. Heat the oil in a small, deep skillet over
   medium-high heat until shimmering. Fry the shallots in batches,
   stirring, for 2-4 minutes until deep golden. Lift out with a
   slotted spoon onto paper towels. They crisp further as they
   cool. (Can be made 1 day ahead; store airtight at room
   temperature.)
2. Blanch the beans. Cook the green beans in a large pot of
   well-salted boiling water for 5 minutes until bright green and
   just tender. Drain and plunge into ice water to stop the
   cooking. Drain well and pat dry.
3. Make the sauce. Melt the butter in a large skillet over
   medium-high heat. Add the mushrooms and cook, stirring
   occasionally, for 8-10 minutes until they release their liquid
   and turn golden. Add the garlic and thyme and cook 1 minute.
   Sprinkle in the flour and stir for 1 minute.
4. Slowly stir in the stock, then the milk. Simmer, stirring, for
   4-6 minutes until thick and creamy. Season with nutmeg, soy
   sauce, salt and pepper.
5. Assemble. Heat the oven to 375°F (190°C). Fold the beans into
   the sauce along with half of the crispy shallots. Spread in a
   buttered 9x13-inch (23x33 cm) or 3-quart baking dish.
6. Bake for 15-20 minutes until bubbling. Scatter the remaining
   shallots on top and bake 5 minutes more.

MAKE AHEAD
  - Assemble through step 5 (without any shallots) up to 2 days
    ahead; cover and refrigerate. Bake for 30-35 minutes, stirring
    in half the shallots partway through, then top with the rest
    for the final 5 minutes.

1.7 SILKY PUMPKIN PIE
----------------------------------------------------------------
Makes: one 9-inch (23 cm) pie, 8-10 slices
Prep: 30 minutes (plus chilling)  |  Bake: 65-75 minutes total
Cool: at least 3 hours

An all-butter crust and a smooth, warmly spiced custard that sets
without cracking.

INGREDIENTS
All-butter crust
  - 1 1/4 cups (160 g) all-purpose flour
  - 1 teaspoon granulated sugar
  - 1/2 teaspoon salt
  - 1/2 cup (1 stick / 115 g) very cold unsalted butter, cubed
  - 3-5 tablespoons ice water

Filling
  - 1 can (15 oz / 425 g) pure pumpkin puree (not pumpkin pie
    filling), or 1 3/4 cups homemade puree
  - 3/4 cup (150 g) packed light brown sugar
  - 1 1/2 teaspoons ground cinnamon
  - 1 teaspoon ground ginger
  - 1/4 teaspoon ground nutmeg
  - 1/4 teaspoon ground cloves or allspice
  - 1/2 teaspoon salt
  - 3 large eggs, plus 1 egg yolk
  - 1 cup (240 ml) heavy cream
  - 1/2 cup (120 ml) whole milk
  - 1 teaspoon vanilla extract

To serve
  - Lightly sweetened whipped cream

METHOD
1. Make the dough. Whisk the flour, sugar and salt. Cut in the
   butter with a pastry cutter or your fingertips until the
   mixture looks like coarse crumbs with some pea-sized pieces.
   Drizzle in ice water a tablespoon at a time, tossing with a
   fork, until the dough holds together when squeezed. Press into
   a disk, wrap and refrigerate at least 1 hour (or up to 2 days).
2. Shape. Roll the dough into a 12-inch (30 cm) circle on a
   floured surface. Ease it into a 9-inch pie plate, trim to a
   1/2-inch overhang, fold under and crimp the edge. Freeze for
   20 minutes.
3. Blind bake. Heat the oven to 400°F (200°C). Line the chilled
   crust with parchment and fill with pie weights or dried beans.
   Bake 18-20 minutes until the edges are set. Remove the
   parchment and weights and bake 5-7 minutes more until the
   bottom looks dry. Reduce the oven to 350°F (175°C).
4. Make the filling. While the crust bakes, whisk the pumpkin,
   brown sugar, spices and salt in a saucepan over medium heat
   for 4-5 minutes until it darkens slightly and starts to sputter
   (this deepens the flavor). Remove from the heat and whisk in
   the cream and milk, then let cool for 5 minutes. Whisk in the
   eggs, yolk and vanilla until completely smooth.
5. Bake. Pour the filling into the warm crust. Bake at 350°F
   (175°C) for 40-50 minutes, until the edges are set and the
   center still wobbles slightly like gelatin when you nudge the
   pan (about 175°F / 80°C in the center). If the crust edge
   browns too fast, cover it with a foil ring.
6. Cool. Cool on a wire rack for at least 3 hours. The center
   finishes setting as it cools; cooling slowly helps prevent
   cracks. Serve at room temperature or chilled, with whipped
   cream.

MAKE AHEAD
  - Bake up to 2 days ahead. Once cool, cover and refrigerate.
  - Short on time? Use a good-quality store-bought crust and
    start at step 3.

2. THE TURKEY GUIDE: THAWING, COOKING AND CARVING
================================================================

----------------------------------------------------------------
HOW BIG A TURKEY DO I NEED?
----------------------------------------------------------------
Plan on 1 to 1 1/2 lb (450-680 g) of whole turkey per person.
Use the higher amount if you want leftovers.

  Guests      Turkey size
  ------      -----------
  4-6         8-10 lb (3.5-4.5 kg)
  6-8         10-12 lb (4.5-5.5 kg)
  8-10        12-14 lb (5.5-6.5 kg)
  10-12       14-16 lb (6.5-7.5 kg)
  12-14       16-18 lb (7.5-8 kg)
  15-20       20-24 lb (9-11 kg), or two smaller birds

----------------------------------------------------------------
THAWING A FROZEN TURKEY
----------------------------------------------------------------
Never thaw a turkey at room temperature on the counter. The
outside can sit in the bacterial "danger zone" (40-140°F /
4-60°C) for hours while the center is still frozen.
Use one of these three safe methods.

METHOD 1: IN THE REFRIGERATOR (recommended)
  - Allow about 24 hours for every 4-5 lb (2-2.25 kg) of turkey.
  - Keep the turkey in its original wrapper, breast-side up, in a
    rimmed tray or roasting pan on the bottom shelf to catch drips.
  - Once thawed, it keeps safely in the fridge for 1-2 more days.

  Turkey weight          Refrigerator thawing time
  -------------          -------------------------
  4-12 lb (2-5.5 kg)     1 to 3 days
  12-16 lb (5.5-7 kg)    3 to 4 days
  16-20 lb (7-9 kg)      4 to 5 days
  20-24 lb (9-11 kg)     5 to 6 days

METHOD 2: IN COLD WATER (faster, more hands-on)
  - Allow about 30 minutes per pound (about 1 hour per kg).
  - Keep the turkey in a leak-proof wrapper or sealed bag and
    submerge it breast-side down in a clean sink or cooler of
    cold tap water.
  - Change the water every 30 minutes so it stays cold.
  - Cook the turkey immediately after it thaws.

  Turkey weight          Cold-water thawing time
  -------------          -----------------------
  4-12 lb (2-5.5 kg)     2 to 6 hours
  12-16 lb (5.5-7 kg)    6 to 8 hours
  16-20 lb (7-9 kg)      8 to 10 hours
  20-24 lb (9-11 kg)     10 to 12 hours

METHOD 3: IN THE MICROWAVE (small birds only)
  - Only if the turkey fits and your microwave has a defrost
    setting. Follow the manufacturer's instructions.
  - Cook immediately after thawing, as some areas may begin to
    cook in the microwave.

EMERGENCY: STILL FROZEN ON THE DAY?
  - A turkey can be safely roasted from frozen or partly frozen.
    Allow about 50% more cooking time than for a thawed bird.
    Remove the giblet bag with tongs as soon as it loosens
    (usually after about 2 hours of roasting).

HOW TO TELL IT'S THAWED
  - The breast and legs feel soft when pressed, the cavity has no
    ice crystals, and you can easily remove the neck and giblets.

----------------------------------------------------------------
COOKING YOUR TURKEY
----------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
  - Remove the neck and giblet bag from both cavities (check the
    neck end too).
  - Pat the turkey dry for crisper skin.
  - Do not wash the raw turkey. Rinsing does not make it safer;
    it splashes bacteria around your sink and kitchen.
  - Wash hands, boards and surfaces with hot soapy water after
    handling raw poultry.

ROASTING TIMES AT 325°F (165°C)
These are estimates for a thawed bird. Start checking the
temperature 30-45 minutes before the earliest time.

  Turkey weight          Unstuffed               Stuffed
  -------------          ---------               -------
  8-12 lb (3.5-5.5 kg)   2 3/4 to 3 hours        3 to 3 1/2 hours
  12-14 lb (5.5-6.5 kg)  3 to 3 3/4 hours        3 1/2 to 4 hours
  14-18 lb (6.5-8 kg)    3 3/4 to 4 1/4 hours    4 to 4 1/4 hours
  18-20 lb (8-9 kg)      4 1/4 to 4 1/2 hours    4 1/4 to 4 3/4 hours
  20-24 lb (9-11 kg)     4 1/2 to 5 hours        4 3/4 to 5 1/4 hours

THE ONLY RELIABLE TEST: A THERMOMETER
  - Use an instant-read or leave-in probe thermometer.
  - Check three places: the thickest part of the thigh (not
    touching bone), the innermost part of the wing, and the
    thickest part of the breast.
  - The turkey is safely cooked when all three read at least
    165°F (74°C).
  - Pop-up timers are not reliable; double-check with a
    thermometer.

IF YOU STUFF THE BIRD
  - Stuff loosely, just before roasting, with warm, freshly made
    stuffing (never stuff ahead of time).
  - Allow about 3/4 cup stuffing per pound of turkey.
  - The center of the stuffing must also reach 165°F (74°C).
    If the turkey is done but the stuffing isn't, scoop the
    stuffing into a dish and finish it in the oven.
  - For simplicity and safety, we recommend baking stuffing in a
    separate dish (see Recipe 1.2).

KEYS TO A GREAT BIRD
  - Dry brine 1-2 days ahead for seasoned meat and crisp skin.
  - Roast on a rack so hot air circulates underneath.
  - Tent the breast loosely with foil if it browns too quickly.
  - Basting is optional. Opening the oven often lowers the
    temperature and lengthens cooking time.
  - Rest the turkey 30-40 minutes before carving. It stays hot,
    and the juices stay in the meat instead of on the board.

----------------------------------------------------------------
CARVING YOUR TURKEY
----------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT YOU NEED
  - A large cutting board with a groove to catch juices
  - A long, sharp carving or chef's knife
  - A carving fork or tongs
  - A warm serving platter
  - Paper towels for gripping

STEP BY STEP
1. Rest. Let the turkey rest 30-40 minutes. Remove any twine.
2. Remove the legs. Pull a leg away from the body and slice
   through the skin between the leg and the breast. Press the leg
   down and outward until the hip joint pops, then cut through the
   joint to free the whole leg. Repeat on the other side.
3. Separate drumstick and thigh. Place each leg skin-side down
   and find the joint between drumstick and thigh (follow the line
   of fat). Cut straight through the joint.
4. Slice the thigh meat. Cut along the thigh bone to remove the
   meat in one piece, then slice it across the grain. Serve the
   drumsticks whole or slice the meat off them.
5. Remove the wings. Pull each wing away from the body and cut
   through the shoulder joint.
6. Remove the breasts whole. Make a long cut down one side of
   the breastbone. Keeping your knife close to the ribcage, use
   long, smooth strokes to ease the entire breast half away from
   the bone. Repeat on the other side.
7. Slice the breast. Lay each breast half skin-side up and slice
   it crosswise (against the grain) into 1/2-inch (1 cm) slices.
   Every slice gets some crisp skin.
8. Arrange. Fan the breast slices on a warm platter with the dark
   meat, drumsticks and wings around them. Spoon a little warm
   stock or gravy over the meat to keep it moist.
9. Save the carcass for stock or soup.

----------------------------------------------------------------
LEFTOVER SAFETY
----------------------------------------------------------------
  - Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours of cooking.
  - Carve meat off the bone and store in shallow containers so it
    cools quickly.
  - Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3-4 days, or freeze for
    2-6 months.
  - Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C), and bring gravy to a
    rolling boil before serving.

3. THE PREP-AHEAD GUIDE
================================================================

The secret to a calm Thanksgiving is that most of the meal can be
made, or at least started, days ahead. Here is what to make when,
and how to store and reheat it.

----------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT CAN BE MADE AHEAD, AND HOW FAR
----------------------------------------------------------------
UP TO 1 MONTH AHEAD (FREEZER)
  - Turkey stock for gravy: freeze in 2-cup containers.
  - Pie dough: wrap disks well and freeze. Thaw overnight in the
    refrigerator.
  - Bread cubes for stuffing: dry, cool and freeze in bags.
  - Dinner rolls: bake, cool, freeze. Reheat from frozen wrapped
    in foil at 350°F (175°C) for 10-15 minutes.

UP TO 1 WEEK AHEAD
  - Cranberry sauce: refrigerate in a covered container for up to
    10 days.
  - Compound/herb butter: refrigerate up to 1 week.
  - Toasted nuts and spice mixes: store airtight at room
    temperature.

2 TO 3 DAYS AHEAD
  - Gravy base (Recipe 1.4, steps 1-6): refrigerate; add pan
    drippings on the day.
  - Dry brine the turkey: 1-2 days uncovered in the fridge.
  - Chop the "trinity" of onion, celery and carrot for stuffing
    and roasting: store in airtight containers.
  - Pie dough: refrigerate up to 2 days.
  - Mashed potatoes: make fully, store in a buttered baking dish.
  - Green bean casserole: blanch beans and make the sauce;
    assemble and refrigerate (keep the crispy topping separate).

1 DAY AHEAD
  - Bake pies. Pumpkin pie keeps well refrigerated.
  - Make crispy shallots for the green bean casserole.
  - Assemble stuffing (without eggs and stock), cover and
    refrigerate. Add the egg and stock just before baking.
  - Wash and dry salad greens; wrap in paper towels in a bag.
  - Set the table, label serving dishes and set out serving
    utensils.
  - Chill drinks.

THE DAY ITSELF
  - Roast the turkey.
  - Bake stuffing and casserole; reheat mashed potatoes.
  - Finish the gravy with pan drippings.
  - Whip the cream and carve the turkey.

----------------------------------------------------------------
STORAGE AND REHEATING CHEAT SHEET
----------------------------------------------------------------
  Dish                 Store                  Reheat
  ----                 -----                  ------
  Cranberry sauce      Fridge, up to 10 days  Serve cold or room temp
  Gravy                Fridge, 3 days;        Simmer on stovetop,
                       freezer, 3 months      whisking; thin with stock

----------------------------------------------------------------
OVEN-SPACE STRATEGIES
----------------------------------------------------------------
  - The turkey's 30-40 minute rest is your sides window. As soon
    as the turkey comes out, raise the oven to 375°F (190°C) and
    bake the stuffing and casserole.
  - Keep mashed potatoes warm in a slow cooker on LOW.
  - Make gravy on the stovetop.
  - Use a cooler (no ice) lined with towels as a "hot box" to
    hold covered dishes warm for up to an hour.
  - Choose desserts that are served cold or at room temperature.

4. SUBSTITUTIONS AND DIETARY SWAPS
================================================================

----------------------------------------------------------------
EVERYDAY INGREDIENT SUBSTITUTIONS
----------------------------------------------------------------
  Instead of...           Use...
  -------------           ------
  1 cup buttermilk        1 tbsp lemon juice or white vinegar +
                          enough milk to make 1 cup; stand 5 min
  1 cup heavy cream       3/4 cup whole milk + 1/4 cup melted
  (in cooking, not        butter
  for whipping)
  1 cup sour cream        1 cup plain full-fat Greek yogurt
  1 tbsp fresh herbs      1 tsp dried herbs
  1 cup chicken stock     1 cup vegetable stock, or 1 tsp
                          bouillon + 1 cup water
  1 tbsp cornstarch       2 tbsp all-purpose flour
  (for thickening)
  1 cup brown sugar       1 cup white sugar + 1 tbsp molasses
  1 tsp pumpkin pie       1/2 tsp cinnamon + 1/4 tsp ginger +
  spice                   1/8 tsp nutmeg + 1/8 tsp cloves
  Fresh cranberries       Frozen cranberries (no need to thaw)
  Shallots                Yellow onion (use slightly less)
  Fresh pumpkin puree     Canned pure pumpkin (same amount)
  Pie weights             Dried beans or uncooked rice

----------------------------------------------------------------
GLUTEN-FREE
----------------------------------------------------------------
  - Turkey: Naturally gluten-free. Check that any pre-brined bird
    or seasoning blend is labeled gluten-free.
  - Stuffing: Use a sturdy gluten-free bread, or make a
    rice-based stuffing with wild rice, mushrooms and herbs. Use
    gluten-free stock.
  - Gravy: Skip the flour roux. Thicken 4 cups of simmering stock
    with 3 tablespoons cornstarch or arrowroot mixed with 1/4 cup
    cold water. Use tamari instead of soy sauce.
  - Green bean casserole: Thicken the sauce with a gluten-free
    flour blend or sweet rice flour, and coat the shallots in
    rice flour or cornstarch.
  - Pumpkin pie: Use a gluten-free pie crust or a press-in crust
    of 1 1/2 cups almond flour, 3 tbsp melted butter and 2 tbsp
    sugar. Or bake the filling crustless in a buttered dish.
  - Watch for cross-contact: use separate boards, utensils and
    serving spoons.

----------------------------------------------------------------
DAIRY-FREE
----------------------------------------------------------------
  - Butter: Use plant-based butter sticks (or olive oil for
    savory dishes) one-for-one.
  - Turkey: Rub with olive oil and herbs, or use a plant-based
    butter, under the skin.
  - Mashed potatoes: Use olive oil or plant butter with warm
    unsweetened oat milk or the potato cooking water. Roasted
    garlic adds richness.
  - Green bean casserole: Make the sauce with plant butter and
    unsweetened oat or soy milk, or use blended cashews soaked in
    hot water.
  - Pumpkin pie: Replace the cream and milk with 1 1/2 cups
    full-fat canned coconut milk. Use a dairy-free crust.
  - Whipped topping: Chill a can of full-fat coconut milk
    overnight, scoop out the solid cream and whip with a little
    powdered sugar and vanilla.

----------------------------------------------------------------
VEGETARIAN AND VEGAN
----------------------------------------------------------------
  - Main course ideas: a stuffed and roasted whole squash, a
    mushroom and lentil wellington, or a lentil and walnut loaf
    with a maple glaze.
  - Stuffing: Use vegetable stock. For vegan, replace each egg
    with 1 tablespoon ground flaxseed mixed with 3 tablespoons
    water (let it thicken for 5 minutes), or simply add a little
    extra stock.
  - Gravy: Make a mushroom gravy. Brown 1 lb chopped mushrooms
    and an onion in butter or oil, add flour, then whisk in
    vegetable stock with 1 tablespoon soy sauce and fresh thyme.
    Blend for a smooth texture.
  - Green bean casserole: Use vegetable stock (and dairy-free
    swaps for vegan).
  - Pumpkin pie (vegan): Replace the eggs and dairy with 1 1/2
    cups full-fat coconut milk and 3 tablespoons cornstarch. Bake
    until set at the edges with a slight wobble, then chill
    overnight to firm up.

----------------------------------------------------------------
EGG-FREE
----------------------------------------------------------------
  - Stuffing: Leave out the eggs and use 1/2 cup extra stock;
    it will be softer and less set but still delicious.
  - Pumpkin pie: See the vegan option above, or use a
    commercial egg replacer according to the package.

----------------------------------------------------------------
NUT-FREE
----------------------------------------------------------------
  - None of the core recipes require nuts. Skip the pecan and
    walnut variations, and swap toasted pumpkin or sunflower seeds
    for crunch.
  - Check labels on store-bought crusts, breads and crispy
    onions for "may contain" warnings.

----------------------------------------------------------------
LOWER-SODIUM
----------------------------------------------------------------
  - Choose a fresh, not pre-brined or "self-basting," turkey and
    season with herbs, citrus and garlic rather than a salt brine.
  - Use no-salt-added stock and season at the end, to taste.
  - Lean on lemon, vinegar and fresh herbs for brightness.

----------------------------------------------------------------
LIGHTER OPTIONS
----------------------------------------------------------------
  - Mashed potatoes: Swap half the potatoes for steamed
    cauliflower and use Greek yogurt in place of sour cream.
  - Green bean casserole: Use milk instead of cream and bake the
    shallots instead of frying (toss with 1 tbsp oil and bake at
    400°F / 200°C for 15-20 minutes, stirring often).
  - Turkey: Choose white meat and remove the skin on your plate.
  - Pumpkin pie: Use evaporated low-fat milk in place of the
    cream and milk.

----------------------------------------------------------------
SMALL GATHERINGS
----------------------------------------------------------------
  - Swap the whole turkey for a bone-in turkey breast (6-7 lb /
    2.7-3.2 kg) which serves 6-8 and roasts in about 2-2 1/2 hours
    at 325°F (165°C). Cook to 165°F (74°C).
  - Halve the side-dish recipes and bake in an 8x8-inch dish.


================================================================
5. THE THANKSGIVING PLANNER
================================================================

----------------------------------------------------------------
5.1 PREP TIMELINE
----------------------------------------------------------------
Count back from Thanksgiving Day. Adjust if your turkey is larger
than 14 lb, as thawing takes longer.

4 WEEKS BEFORE THANKSGIVING
  - Finalize your guest list and ask about allergies and dietary
    needs.
  - Plan your menu. Decide what you'll make and what guests will
    bring.
  - Order a fresh or heritage turkey if you want one.
  - Check that you have a roasting pan, rack, instant-read
    thermometer, carving knife, and enough plates, glasses,
    chairs and serving dishes. Borrow or buy what's missing.

3 WEEKS BEFORE THANKSGIVING
  - Write your master shopping list, split into non-perishables
    and fresh items.
  - Buy non-perishables: flour, sugar, spices, canned pumpkin,
    stock, oils, wine and drinks.
  - Make and freeze pie dough and turkey stock.

2 WEEKS BEFORE THANKSGIVING
  - Buy a frozen turkey (if using) and store it in the freezer.
  - Make and freeze dinner rolls and dried bread cubes.
  - Plan your oven schedule: list what needs the oven, at what
    temperature, and for how long.
  - Confirm what each guest is bringing.

7 DAYS BEFORE THANKSGIVING
  - Clean out the refrigerator to make room for the turkey and
    make-ahead dishes.
  - Plan serving dishes for each recipe; label them with sticky
    notes.
  - Make cranberry sauce (keeps up to 10 days).

5 DAYS BEFORE THANKSGIVING
  - Move a 16-20 lb frozen turkey to the refrigerator to thaw.
    (For a 20-24 lb turkey, start 6 days before.)

4 DAYS BEFORE THANKSGIVING
  - Move a 12-16 lb frozen turkey to the refrigerator to thaw.
  - Buy fresh produce, dairy and herbs.

3 DAYS BEFORE THANKSGIVING
  - Move a turkey under 12 lb to the refrigerator to thaw.
  - Make the gravy base.
  - Make herb butter.
  - Pick up a fresh turkey if you ordered one.

2 DAYS BEFORE THANKSGIVING
  - Dry brine the turkey (once it's thawed enough to remove the
    giblets).
  - Make mashed potatoes; store in a buttered baking dish.
  - Blanch green beans and make the mushroom sauce; assemble the
    casserole and refrigerate.
  - Chop onions, celery and carrots for stuffing and roasting.
  - Move frozen pie dough to the refrigerator.

1 DAY BEFORE THANKSGIVING
  - Bake the pumpkin pie.
  - Make the crispy shallots.
  - Make the stuffing base (bread, vegetables, herbs); refrigerate.
  - Set the table and lay out serving dishes and utensils.
  - Chill drinks; check you have ice.
  - Check that the turkey is fully thawed. If not, finish thawing
    it in cold water.
  - Review the cooking-day schedule and set a few phone alarms.

THANKSGIVING DAY
  - Follow the cooking-day schedule below.
  - Relax and enjoy your guests!

----------------------------------------------------------------
5.2 COOKING-DAY SCHEDULE
----------------------------------------------------------------
Sample schedule for a 12-14 lb turkey with dinner served at
4:00 PM. For a different dinner time, shift every step by the
same amount. For a larger bird, start earlier using the roasting
chart in Section 2.

  Time       Task
  ----       ----
  9:30 AM    Take the turkey out of the refrigerator to sit at
             room temperature for up to 1 hour. Make the herb
             butter if you haven't already.

  10:00 AM   Heat the oven to 425°F (220°C). Prepare the roasting
             pan with vegetables and stock. Butter the turkey.

  10:30 AM   Turkey into the oven at 425°F (220°C).

  11:00 AM   Reduce the oven to 325°F (165°C). Continue roasting.

  11:30 AM   Take the stuffing base, mashed potatoes and green bean
             casserole out of the fridge to lose their chill.
             Take the pie out of the fridge if serving at room
             temperature later.

  12:00 PM   Check the pan; add a little stock if it's dry. Tent
             the breast with foil if it's browning too fast.
             Tidy the kitchen and run the dishwasher.

  1:00 PM    Start checking the turkey's temperature in the thigh
             and breast. Mix the egg and stock into the stuffing
             and spread it in a buttered dish.

  1:30 PM    Put the mashed potatoes in a slow cooker on LOW (or
             plan to reheat them in the oven after the turkey).
             Set out appetizers and drinks.

  2:00 PM    Turkey should be close to done (165°F / 74°C in the
             thigh and breast). Remove from the oven and rest,
             tented with foil. Strain and skim the pan drippings.

  2:15 PM    Raise the oven to 375°F (190°C). Bake the stuffing,
             covered. Put the green bean casserole in alongside
             it. (Mashed potatoes go in now if reheating in the
             oven.)

  2:40 PM    Uncover the stuffing. Stir half the crispy shallots
             into the casserole.

  3:00 PM    Reheat the gravy base on the stove and whisk in the
             pan drippings. Season to taste. Keep warm on low.

  3:15 PM    Top the casserole with the rest of the shallots and
             bake 5 more minutes. Check the stuffing is golden and
             165°F (74°C) in the center.

  3:30 PM    Carve the turkey and arrange on a warm platter. Stir
             the mashed potatoes and add a splash of warm milk.
             Move cranberry sauce to a serving bowl.

  3:45 PM    Transfer sides to serving dishes. Pour gravy into a
             warm gravy boat. Warm rolls in the oven for
             10 minutes. Whip the cream for the pie and refrigerate.

  4:00 PM    Dinner is served!

  After      Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Slice the pie and
  dinner     serve with whipped cream. Save the carcass for stock.

TIPS FOR A SMOOTH DAY
  - Write the schedule on paper and tape it to a cabinet.
  - Set phone timers for every oven step.
  - Clean as you go, and start with an empty dishwasher.
  - Delegate: guests can pour drinks, set out appetizers, mash
    potatoes or clear plates.
  - If the turkey is done early, that's fine. Rested and tented
    in foil, it stays hot for up to an hour.
