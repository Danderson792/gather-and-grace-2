Everyone deserves a place at the table

Swaps that work.

Simple substitution ideas for different needs and preferences—without losing the flavors that feel like Thanksgiving.

Gluten-Free

  • Turkey: Naturally gluten-free. Check that any pre-brined bird or seasoning blend is labeled gluten-free.
  • Stuffing: Use a sturdy gluten-free bread, or make a rice-based stuffing with wild rice, mushrooms and herbs. Use gluten-free stock.
  • Gravy: Skip the flour roux. Thicken 4 cups of simmering stock with 3 tablespoons cornstarch or arrowroot mixed with 1/4 cup cold water. Use tamari instead of soy sauce.
  • Green bean casserole: Thicken the sauce with a gluten-free flour blend or sweet rice flour, and coat the shallots in rice flour or cornstarch.
  • Pumpkin pie: Use a gluten-free pie crust or a press-in crust of 1 1/2 cups almond flour, 3 tbsp melted butter and 2 tbsp sugar. Or bake the filling crustless in a buttered dish.
  • Watch for cross-contact: use separate boards, utensils and serving spoons.

Dairy-Free

  • Butter: Use plant-based butter sticks (or olive oil for savory dishes) one-for-one.
  • Turkey: Rub with olive oil and herbs, or use a plant-based butter, under the skin.
  • Mashed potatoes: Use olive oil or plant butter with warm unsweetened oat milk or the potato cooking water. Roasted garlic adds richness.
  • Green bean casserole: Make the sauce with plant butter and unsweetened oat or soy milk, or use blended cashews soaked in hot water.
  • Pumpkin pie: Replace the cream and milk with 1 1/2 cups full-fat canned coconut milk. Use a dairy-free crust.
  • Whipped topping: Chill a can of full-fat coconut milk overnight, scoop out the solid cream and whip with a little powdered sugar and vanilla.

Vegetarian And Vegan

  • Main course ideas: a stuffed and roasted whole squash, a mushroom and lentil wellington, or a lentil and walnut loaf with a maple glaze.
  • Stuffing: Use vegetable stock. For vegan, replace each egg with 1 tablespoon ground flaxseed mixed with 3 tablespoons water (let it thicken for 5 minutes), or simply add a little extra stock.
  • Gravy: Make a mushroom gravy. Brown 1 lb chopped mushrooms and an onion in butter or oil, add flour, then whisk in vegetable stock with 1 tablespoon soy sauce and fresh thyme. Blend for a smooth texture.
  • Green bean casserole: Use vegetable stock (and dairy-free swaps for vegan).
  • Pumpkin pie (vegan): Replace the eggs and dairy with 1 1/2 cups full-fat coconut milk and 3 tablespoons cornstarch. Bake until set at the edges with a slight wobble, then chill overnight to firm up.

Egg-Free

  • Stuffing: Leave out the eggs and use 1/2 cup extra stock; it will be softer and less set but still delicious.
  • Pumpkin pie: See the vegan option above, or use a commercial egg replacer according to the package.

Nut-Free

  • None of the core recipes require nuts. Skip the pecan and walnut variations, and swap toasted pumpkin or sunflower seeds for crunch.
  • Check labels on store-bought crusts, breads and crispy onions for "may contain" warnings.

Lower-Sodium

  • Choose a fresh, not pre-brined or "self-basting," turkey and season with herbs, citrus and garlic rather than a salt brine.
  • Use no-salt-added stock and season at the end, to taste.
  • Lean on lemon, vinegar and fresh herbs for brightness.

Lighter Options

  • Mashed potatoes: Swap half the potatoes for steamed cauliflower and use Greek yogurt in place of sour cream.
  • Green bean casserole: Use milk instead of cream and bake the shallots instead of frying (toss with 1 tbsp oil and bake at 400°F / 200°C for 15-20 minutes, stirring often).
  • Turkey: Choose white meat and remove the skin on your plate.
  • Pumpkin pie: Use evaporated low-fat milk in place of the cream and milk.

Small Gatherings

  • Swap the whole turkey for a bone-in turkey breast (6-7 lb / 2.7-3.2 kg) which serves 6-8 and roasts in about 2-2 1/2 hours at 325°F (165°C). Cook to 165°F (74°C).
  • Halve the side-dish recipes and bake in an 8x8-inch dish.
