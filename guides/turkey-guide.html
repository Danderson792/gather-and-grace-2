Your turkey, from frozen to fabulous

The complete turkey guide.

A clear path through safe thawing, roasting and carving—plus a little kitchen confidence.

How Big A Turkey Do I Need?

Plan on 1 to 1 1/2 lb (450-680 g) of whole turkey per person. Use the higher amount if you want leftovers.

  Guests      Turkey size
  ------      -----------
  4-6         8-10 lb (3.5-4.5 kg)
  6-8         10-12 lb (4.5-5.5 kg)
  8-10        12-14 lb (5.5-6.5 kg)
  10-12       14-16 lb (6.5-7.5 kg)
  12-14       16-18 lb (7.5-8 kg)
  15-20       20-24 lb (9-11 kg), or two smaller birds

Thawing A Frozen Turkey

Never thaw a turkey at room temperature on the counter. The outside can sit in the bacterial "danger zone" (40-140°F / 4-60°C) for hours while the center is still frozen. Use one of these three safe methods.

METHOD 1: IN THE REFRIGERATOR (recommended)

  • Allow about 24 hours for every 4-5 lb (2-2.25 kg) of turkey.
  • Keep the turkey in its original wrapper, breast-side up, in a rimmed tray or roasting pan on the bottom shelf to catch drips.
  • Once thawed, it keeps safely in the fridge for 1-2 more days.
  Turkey weight          Refrigerator thawing time
  -------------          -------------------------
  4-12 lb (2-5.5 kg)     1 to 3 days
  12-16 lb (5.5-7 kg)    3 to 4 days
  16-20 lb (7-9 kg)      4 to 5 days
  20-24 lb (9-11 kg)     5 to 6 days

METHOD 2: IN COLD WATER (faster, more hands-on)

  • Allow about 30 minutes per pound (about 1 hour per kg).
  • Keep the turkey in a leak-proof wrapper or sealed bag and submerge it breast-side down in a clean sink or cooler of cold tap water.
  • Change the water every 30 minutes so it stays cold.
  • Cook the turkey immediately after it thaws.
  Turkey weight          Cold-water thawing time
  -------------          -----------------------
  4-12 lb (2-5.5 kg)     2 to 6 hours
  12-16 lb (5.5-7 kg)    6 to 8 hours
  16-20 lb (7-9 kg)      8 to 10 hours
  20-24 lb (9-11 kg)     10 to 12 hours

METHOD 3: IN THE MICROWAVE (small birds only)

  • Only if the turkey fits and your microwave has a defrost setting. Follow the manufacturer's instructions.
  • Cook immediately after thawing, as some areas may begin to cook in the microwave.

EMERGENCY: STILL FROZEN ON THE DAY?

  • A turkey can be safely roasted from frozen or partly frozen. Allow about 50% more cooking time than for a thawed bird. Remove the giblet bag with tongs as soon as it loosens (usually after about 2 hours of roasting).

HOW TO TELL IT'S THAWED

  • The breast and legs feel soft when pressed, the cavity has no ice crystals, and you can easily remove the neck and giblets.

Cooking Your Turkey

Before You Start

  • Remove the neck and giblet bag from both cavities (check the neck end too).
  • Pat the turkey dry for crisper skin.
  • Do not wash the raw turkey. Rinsing does not make it safer; it splashes bacteria around your sink and kitchen.
  • Wash hands, boards and surfaces with hot soapy water after handling raw poultry.

ROASTING TIMES AT 325°F (165°C) These are estimates for a thawed bird. Start checking the temperature 30-45 minutes before the earliest time.

  Turkey weight          Unstuffed               Stuffed
  -------------          ---------               -------
  8-12 lb (3.5-5.5 kg)   2 3/4 to 3 hours        3 to 3 1/2 hours
  12-14 lb (5.5-6.5 kg)  3 to 3 3/4 hours        3 1/2 to 4 hours
  14-18 lb (6.5-8 kg)    3 3/4 to 4 1/4 hours    4 to 4 1/4 hours
  18-20 lb (8-9 kg)      4 1/4 to 4 1/2 hours    4 1/4 to 4 3/4 hours
  20-24 lb (9-11 kg)     4 1/2 to 5 hours        4 3/4 to 5 1/4 hours

The Only Reliable Test: A Thermometer

  • Use an instant-read or leave-in probe thermometer.
  • Check three places: the thickest part of the thigh (not touching bone), the innermost part of the wing, and the thickest part of the breast.
  • The turkey is safely cooked when all three read at least 165°F (74°C).
  • Pop-up timers are not reliable; double-check with a thermometer.

If You Stuff The Bird

  • Stuff loosely, just before roasting, with warm, freshly made stuffing (never stuff ahead of time).
  • Allow about 3/4 cup stuffing per pound of turkey.
  • The center of the stuffing must also reach 165°F (74°C). If the turkey is done but the stuffing isn't, scoop the stuffing into a dish and finish it in the oven.
  • For simplicity and safety, we recommend baking stuffing in a separate dish (see Recipe 1.2).

Keys To A Great Bird

  • Dry brine 1-2 days ahead for seasoned meat and crisp skin.
  • Roast on a rack so hot air circulates underneath.
  • Tent the breast loosely with foil if it browns too quickly.
  • Basting is optional. Opening the oven often lowers the temperature and lengthens cooking time.
  • Rest the turkey 30-40 minutes before carving. It stays hot, and the juices stay in the meat instead of on the board.

Carving Your Turkey

What You Need

  • A large cutting board with a groove to catch juices
  • A long, sharp carving or chef's knife
  • A carving fork or tongs
  • A warm serving platter
  • Paper towels for gripping

Step By Step

  1. Rest. Let the turkey rest 30-40 minutes. Remove any twine.
  2. Remove the legs. Pull a leg away from the body and slice through the skin between the leg and the breast. Press the leg down and outward until the hip joint pops, then cut through the joint to free the whole leg. Repeat on the other side.
  3. Separate drumstick and thigh. Place each leg skin-side down and find the joint between drumstick and thigh (follow the line of fat). Cut straight through the joint.
  4. Slice the thigh meat. Cut along the thigh bone to remove the meat in one piece, then slice it across the grain. Serve the drumsticks whole or slice the meat off them.
  5. Remove the wings. Pull each wing away from the body and cut through the shoulder joint.
  6. Remove the breasts whole. Make a long cut down one side of the breastbone. Keeping your knife close to the ribcage, use long, smooth strokes to ease the entire breast half away from the bone. Repeat on the other side.
  7. Slice the breast. Lay each breast half skin-side up and slice it crosswise (against the grain) into 1/2-inch (1 cm) slices. Every slice gets some crisp skin.
  8. Arrange. Fan the breast slices on a warm platter with the dark meat, drumsticks and wings around them. Spoon a little warm stock or gravy over the meat to keep it moist.
  9. Save the carcass for stock or soup.

Leftover Safety

  • Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours of cooking.
  • Carve meat off the bone and store in shallow containers so it cools quickly.
  • Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3-4 days, or freeze for 2-6 months.
  • Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C), and bring gravy to a rolling boil before serving.
