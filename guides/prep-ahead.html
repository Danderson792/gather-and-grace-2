Give tomorrow a head start

Prep ahead, stress less.

A handful of small jobs today can make Thanksgiving feel like a celebration instead of a sprint.

The secret to a calm Thanksgiving is preparing dishes ahead. Use this detailed timing and storage guide to plan the days leading up to the feast.

What Can Be Made Ahead, And How Far

Up To 1 Month Ahead (Freezer)

  • Turkey stock for gravy: freeze in 2-cup containers.
  • Pie dough: wrap disks well and freeze. Thaw overnight in the refrigerator.
  • Bread cubes for stuffing: dry, cool and freeze in bags.
  • Dinner rolls: bake, cool, freeze. Reheat from frozen wrapped in foil at 350°F (175°C) for 10-15 minutes.

Up To 1 Week Ahead

  • Cranberry sauce: refrigerate in a covered container for up to 10 days.
  • Compound/herb butter: refrigerate up to 1 week.
  • Toasted nuts and spice mixes: store airtight at room temperature.

2 TO 3 DAYS AHEAD

  • Gravy base (Recipe 1.4, steps 1-6): refrigerate; add pan drippings on the day.
  • Dry brine the turkey: 1-2 days uncovered in the fridge.
  • Chop the "trinity" of onion, celery and carrot for stuffing and roasting: store in airtight containers.
  • Pie dough: refrigerate up to 2 days.
  • Mashed potatoes: make fully, store in a buttered baking dish.
  • Green bean casserole: blanch beans and make the sauce; assemble and refrigerate (keep the crispy topping separate).

1 DAY AHEAD

  • Bake pies. Pumpkin pie keeps well refrigerated.
  • Make crispy shallots for the green bean casserole.
  • Assemble stuffing (without eggs and stock), cover and refrigerate. Add the egg and stock just before baking.
  • Wash and dry salad greens; wrap in paper towels in a bag.
  • Set the table, label serving dishes and set out serving utensils.
  • Chill drinks.

The Day Itself

  • Roast the turkey.
  • Bake stuffing and casserole; reheat mashed potatoes.
  • Finish the gravy with pan drippings.
  • Whip the cream and carve the turkey.

Oven-Space Strategies

  • The turkey's 30-40 minute rest is your sides window. As soon as the turkey comes out, raise the oven to 375°F (190°C) and bake the stuffing and casserole.
  • Keep mashed potatoes warm in a slow cooker on LOW.
  • Make gravy on the stovetop.
  • Use a cooler (no ice) lined with towels as a "hot box" to hold covered dishes warm for up to an hour.
  • Choose desserts that are served cold or at room temperature.
